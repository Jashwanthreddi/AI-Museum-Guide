Title: Museum Collections

The collections of the Salar Jung Museum reflect the evolution of human civilization, ranging from the 2nd century B.C. to the early 20th century A.D. The museum houses:
- Over 46,000 Art Objects
- Over 8,000 Manuscripts
- Over 60,000 Printed Books

These are displayed across more than 38 galleries and categorized into:
- Indian Art
- Middle Eastern Art
- Persian Art
- Nepalese Art
- Japanese Art
- Chinese Art
- Western Art

A dedicated gallery, “The Founder’s Gallery,” highlights the legacy of the Salar Jung family.

Title: History of Salar Jung Museum

The Salar Jung Museum was established in 1951, located on the southern bank of the River Musi in Hyderabad, Telangana, India. Its priceless collection was amassed by the Salar Jung family — one of the most distinguished families in Deccan history, five of whom served as prime ministers under the Nizams of Hyderabad.

Nawab Mir Yousuf Ali Khan, known as Salar Jung III, was appointed prime minister in 1912 by Nizam VII, Nawab Mir Osman Ali Khan. He relinquished the post in 1914 and spent the rest of his life acquiring art and rare manuscripts. His love for art became well known; his residence, Dewan Deodi, was a hub for antique dealers from around the world. He employed agents abroad and personally collected during travels to Europe and the Middle East.

Salar Jung III passed away on March 2, 1949, leaving behind a vast, uncatalogued collection without an heir. His family generously donated the collection to the nation.

The museum opened to the public on 16th December 1951 in Dewan Deodi, inaugurated by India’s first Prime Minister, Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru. Later, the Government of India formally took over the museum. In 1961, the museum was declared an “Institution of National Importance” by an Act of Parliament.

In 1968, it was shifted to its present building, inaugurated by President Dr. Zakir Hussain. Governance was handed to an autonomous board, with the Governor of Andhra Pradesh as chairman — now, post-bifurcation, the Governor of Telangana.

Title: The Salar Jung Family Legacy

**Salar Jung I – Nawab Mir Turab Ali Khan**  
Awarded the title "Salar Jung Bahadur" at age 13. Became Prime Minister of Hyderabad at 24 under Nizam IV. A reformer, art connoisseur, and early patron of European arts. On his 1876 visit to England, he commissioned ceramics with his own portraits and brought back iconic artworks, including the museum’s centerpiece: *Veiled Rebecca*. He died in 1883.

**Salar Jung II – Nawab Mir Laiq Ali Khan**  
Eldest son of Salar Jung I. Appointed Prime Minister in 1884 under Nizam VI, known for continuing his father’s social reforms. Died young at Poona at age 26, leaving behind an infant son: Abdul Qasim Mir Yousuf Ali Khan.

**Salar Jung III – Nawab Mir Yousuf Ali Khan**  
Inherited the title “Salar Jung” at age ten. Educated with utmost care and sophistication. Heir to a vast estate of 450 villages and an annual income of Rs. 23 lakh, he was both wealthy and artistically inclined.

From a young age, Salar Jung III was passionate about art, acquiring rare and exquisite items from India, Europe, the Middle East, and the Far East. He emulated the traditions of European nobility by commissioning custom gold-crested crockery and cutlery from renowned European manufacturers — many of which are now housed in the museum.

He was appointed Prime Minister in 1912, served briefly, then resigned in 1914 due to health issues. The remaining 35 years of his life were devoted to collecting and preserving art and literature, culminating in one of the most spectacular private collections in India’s history.

Title: Salar Jung Museum Library Overview

- The library was opened to the public in 1961 by an Act of Parliament.
- It houses one of the richest collections of manuscripts in the world.
- The collection features ornate embellishments using gold, mineral colors, lapis lazuli (blue), pearl (white), shangraf (red), and Zabarjad/emerald (green).
- Artists involved: Calligraphers, bookbinders, illustrators—each showcasing exceptional craftsmanship.

---

Title: Library History and Collection

- The origins of the collection trace back to 1656 A.D.
- The library was shaped into a modern institution by Salar Jung I, II, and III.
- Location: 2nd floor of the museum.
- Total printed books: **62,772**
  - English: 41,208
  - Urdu: 13,027
  - Hindi: 1,108
  - Telugu: 1,105
  - Persian: 3,576
  - Arabic: 2,588
  - Turkish: 160
- The collection includes religious texts, scientific works, literature, and rare historical documents.
- Oldest English book: *General History of the Turks* (Richard Knolles, 1631).

Title: Manuscript Collection

- Media: Parchment, textile, palm leaf, paper, glass, wood, stone
- Languages: Arabic, Persian, Urdu, Turkish, Dakhni, Pushtu, Hindi, Sanskrit, Telugu, Oriya
- Subjects: Over 84 including:
  - Medicine, Logic, Calligraphy, Mathematics, Astronomy, Music, Poetry, Theology, Law, Ethics, Travel, Magic, Archery, etc.
- Religious diversity: Islam, Hinduism, Christianity, Zoroastrianism
- Special Items:
  - Smallest Quran (2.4 cm) and largest Quran (60 x 30 cm)
  - Nail-work calligraphy and glass-incised panels
  - Scripts: Kufti, Thulth, Naskh, Ta'liq, Nasta'aliq, Gubar, Shikasta, etc.

- Total: Over 2,500

  - *Hayatul Haiwan* – Natural history
